    Affects¶

    IRI https://w3id.org/crima-ontology/ich#Affects
    Description

    'Affects' connection of an Impact Chain model. Indicates a generic relationship where one element is supposed to alter (in intensity or geographic extent) the second one in some way (not necessarily based on a causal link). Links a Vulnerability to a Impact/Risk factor. Example: Lack of land ownership (Vulnerability) affects the disruption of agricultural livelihoods in a flood (Impact).

    Sub Class Of Impact Chain connection c
    Restriction
    • source op some Vulnerability factor c
    • target op some None:n2a5f57d8c3ed4ae6977efd3687c41543b21 c

    connection time lag¶

    IRI https://w3id.org/crima-ontology/ich#ConnectionTimeLag
    Description

    Enumeration of time lag values. Applicable in the context of a connection between two factors. Refers to the time lapse between the incidence of the source factor and the subsequent factor, especially in relation to the interactions from hazard to hazard, hazard to impact, and impact to impact. Example for Increased in extreme heat -> Increased in heat stress: Although the response to heat is immediate, prolonged exposure time is required to generate heat stress, and this duration can vary from hours to days, depending on the element exposed.

    Sub Class Of Concept c

    Exposure factor¶

    IRI https://w3id.org/crima-ontology/ich#ExposureFactor
    Is Defined By

    No standard reference, see IPCC, UNDRR (https://www.undrr.org/terminology/exposure), Prevention web (https://www.preventionweb.net/understanding-disaster-risk/component-risk/exposure), Charim or Climate Risk Sourcebook (https://reliefweb.int/report/world/climate-risk-sourcebook).

    Description

    • Exposure factor of an Impact Chain model.

    • The (presence of) people, livelihoods, species or ecosystems, environmental functions, services, and resources, infrastructure, or economic, social, or cultural assets in places and settings that could be adversely affected (IPCC, 2021).

    Is Defined By https://reliefweb.int/report/world/climate-risk-sourcebook
    Description

    • Impact factor of an Impact Chain model.

    • Effect or consequence caused by a hazard or another impact. Within an Impact Chain model, Impacts relate to adverse (negative) consequences that can be considered as potential materializations of risks.

    Impacts on¶

    IRI https://w3id.org/crima-ontology/ich#ImpactsOn
    Description

    'Impacts on' connection of an Impact Chain model. Indicates mainly a relationship between an impact and an exposed asset. Links an Impact factor to an Exposure factor. Example: An increase in power outages (Impact) impacts industries (Exposure).

    Leads to¶

    IRI https://w3id.org/crima-ontology/ich#LeadsTo
    Description

    'Leads to' connection of an Impact Chain model. Indicates a relationship where one factor contributes to another (often indicating a causal relationship). Links a Hazard to a Hazard/Impact factor, or an Impact to another Impact factor. Example: An increase in average temperature (Hazard) leads to the melting of glaciers (Impact).

    Sub Class Of Impact Chain connection c

    Mitigates¶

    IRI https://w3id.org/crima-ontology/ich#Mitigates
    Description

    'Mitigates' connection of an Impact Chain model. Indicates a relationship that aims to reduce vulnerability or impacts. Links an Adaptation factor to a Vulnerability/Impact factor. Example: An improved irrigation technique (adaptation measure) mitigates the decrease in crop yield (impact) during droughts.

    Sub Class Of Impact Chain connection c

    Underlying risk driver factor¶

    IRI https://w3id.org/crima-ontology/ich#UnderlyingRiskDriverFactor
    Is Defined By

    PAR, Climate Risk Sourcebook (https://reliefweb.int/report/world/climate-risk-sourcebook - Zebisch et al., 2023) and Forin (https://www.irdrinternational.org/pdf/uploads/files/p0Rm0Zgt1EyRt4vABHAn4IOUXCig6WE5xi9iVri9.pdf).

    Description

    • Underlying risk driver factor of an Impact Chain model.

    • It refers to the (non-climatic) risk drivers that may significantly alter the socio-economic or environmental situations and possibly amplify the negative consequences of impacts by acting on their determinants (e.g., vulnerability or exposure) but cannot be mitigated or controlled within the scope of the Impact Chain model.

    Vulnerability factor¶

    IRI https://w3id.org/crima-ontology/ich#VulnerabilityFactor
    Is Defined By

    INFORM.MOVE Framework (https://drmkc.jrc.ec.europa.eu/inform-index/INFORM-Risk/Methodology - Birkmann et al., 2013) (both for risk assessment but incl. Classification of vulnerability components).

    Description

    • Vulnerability factor of an Impact Chain model.

    • Vulnerability refers to the predisposition/propensity of exposed elements such as human beings, their livelihoods, and assets to be adversely affected by a natural hazard. This element contributes to and encompasses a variety of concepts and elements, including sensitivity or susceptibility to harm and/or lack of capacity to prevent, prepare, respond, cope and/or adapt (IPCC, 2021).
